Foundation Service

SAM Registration

The System for Award Management (SAM) is the official U.S. government system that consolidated federal procurement and award systems. Registration is mandatory for any business wanting to do business with the federal government, receive federal grants, or be considered for contract opportunities.

Why SAM Registration Matters

Without an active SAM registration, your business cannot receive contract awards, be paid for government work, or appear in government databases that contracting officers use to find vendors. It's the gateway to the entire federal marketplace.

Beyond federal contracting, SAM registration provides access to Grants.gov for federal grant opportunities and GovLoans.gov for government-backed financing programs. Many state and local governments also require or prefer SAM-registered vendors.

What Gets Registered

SAM registration creates your official business profile with the federal government. This includes your company's legal information, banking details for payment, points of contact, and most importantly, your NAICS codes that define what services or products you provide.

  • Entity Information: Legal business name, DBA names, physical address, and business structure
  • CAGE Code: Commercial and Government Entity code assigned by the Defense Logistics Agency
  • NAICS Codes: North American Industry Classification System codes that categorize your business capabilities
  • Size Standards: Small business designations based on your NAICS codes and revenue/employee count
  • Banking Information: Account details for receiving electronic payments from the government
  • Points of Contact: Government business, electronic business, and past performance contacts
  • Socioeconomic Status: Self-certifications for applicable programs (woman-owned, veteran-owned, etc.)

Common SAM Registration Mistakes We Prevent

  • Incorrect NAICS codes that limit your contract opportunities
  • Missing size standards that disqualify you from small business set-asides
  • Incomplete representations and certifications
  • Banking errors that delay contract payments
  • Expired registrations that make your bids non-compliant

Timeline and Maintenance

New SAM registrations typically take 2-4 weeks for government processing and activation. During this time, the government validates your information, assigns your CAGE code (if new), and activates your profile in the system.

SAM registrations must be renewed annually. An expired registration means you cannot receive new awards or payments. We track your renewal dates and handle the entire update process, ensuring your registration stays current and accurate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does SAM registration cost?

SAM registration itself is free through the government. Be wary of third-party services charging excessive fees. We include SAM registration as part of our programs or offer it as a standalone service for a reasonable fee that covers our time and expertise in ensuring it's done correctly.

Can I register in SAM myself?

Yes, but the process is complex and mistakes are common. Incorrect NAICS codes, missing certifications, or data entry errors can limit your opportunities or cause registration rejections. Our expertise ensures your registration is optimized for maximum contract opportunities.

What if my registration is rejected?

Common rejection reasons include unverified banking information, IRS TIN/EIN mismatches, or incomplete documentation. We work directly with SAM support to resolve any issues and get your registration activated as quickly as possible.

Competitive Advantage

SBA Certifications

Small Business Administration certifications open doors to billions in set-aside contracts reserved specifically for qualifying businesses. These certifications verify your eligibility for programs designed to help disadvantaged, woman-owned, veteran-owned, and businesses in underserved areas compete for government work.

Why Certifications Matter

The federal government has small business contracting goals that require agencies to award a percentage of contracts to certified small businesses. In fiscal year 2023, the federal government awarded over $178 billion to small businesses. Many of these contracts are set-aside exclusively for certified businesses, meaning you're not competing against large corporations.

Certifications also provide access to sole-source contracts up to certain thresholds, meaning agencies can award contracts directly to your business without full competition. This dramatically increases your chances of winning work.

Certification Programs We Support

8(a) Business Development

Small Disadvantaged Business Program

The most comprehensive federal small business program, providing access to sole-source contracts up to $4.5 million, business development assistance, and mentorship opportunities over a 9-year term.

Key Requirements
  • 51%+ owned by socially/economically disadvantaged individual(s)
  • Personal net worth under $850,000
  • Business operating for 2+ years
  • Good character determination

HUBZone

Historically Underutilized Business Zone

For businesses located in and employing residents from economically distressed areas. Provides 10% price evaluation preference and access to HUBZone set-aside contracts.

Key Requirements
  • Principal office in designated HUBZone
  • 35%+ employees live in HUBZone
  • 51%+ owned by U.S. citizens
  • Meet SBA size standards

WOSB/EDWOSB

Woman-Owned Small Business

For businesses majority-owned by women. EDWOSB (Economically Disadvantaged) adds additional set-aside opportunities in industries where women are underrepresented.

Key Requirements
  • 51%+ owned by women U.S. citizens
  • Women control management and daily operations
  • Meet SBA size standards
  • EDWOSB: Personal net worth under $850,000

VOSB/SDVOSB

Veteran-Owned Small Business

For businesses owned by veterans. SDVOSB (Service-Disabled) provides additional advantages including sole-source authority for VA contracts up to $5 million.

Key Requirements
  • 51%+ owned by veteran(s)
  • Veteran controls management and operations
  • SDVOSB: Service-connected disability rating
  • Now verified through SBA VetCert

Our Certification Process

Obtaining SBA certification requires extensive documentation, narrative responses, and careful attention to eligibility requirements. The SBA reviews applications thoroughly and requests additional information frequently. Our process ensures your application is complete and compelling:

  • Eligibility Assessment: We evaluate your business against all program requirements before beginning the application
  • Document Preparation: Gathering and organizing all required corporate, financial, and personal documents
  • Narrative Development: Writing compelling responses that demonstrate your qualifications
  • Application Submission: Completing the certification portal submission with all supporting materials
  • Response Management: Handling SBA requests for additional information or clarification
  • Annual Reviews: Supporting ongoing compliance and renewal requirements

Certification Timeline Expectations

SBA certification processing times vary by program. 8(a) applications typically take 90+ days. WOSB certifications through SBA average 30-60 days. HUBZone can take 60-90 days. We prepare your application to minimize delays and respond quickly to any SBA inquiries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I have multiple certifications?

Yes! Many businesses qualify for multiple certifications. For example, a woman veteran who owns a business in a HUBZone area could potentially qualify for WOSB, VOSB/SDVOSB, and HUBZone certifications simultaneously, dramatically expanding available opportunities.

What if I don't qualify for any certifications?

Many successful government contractors don't have SBA certifications. You can still compete for full and open contracts, subcontracting opportunities with prime contractors, and state/local contracts. Certifications are an advantage, not a requirement.

How long do certifications last?

8(a) certification lasts 9 years with annual reviews. WOSB requires annual recertification. HUBZone requires recertification every 3 years. VOSB/SDVOSB requires reverification every 3 years. We track all deadlines and handle renewals.

Marketing Essential

Capability Statement

Your capability statement is your business's resume for government contracting. It's the first document contracting officers and prime contractors request when evaluating potential vendors. A professional, well-organized capability statement opens doors and creates opportunities.

What Makes an Effective Capability Statement

Government contracting officers review hundreds of capability statements. They spend an average of 7 seconds on initial review before deciding to read further or move on. Your capability statement must immediately communicate who you are, what you do, and why you're qualified.

Unlike a commercial resume or brochure, a capability statement follows government conventions and includes specific information that contracting officers need to evaluate your company as a potential vendor.

Essential Components

  • Core Competencies: 3-5 specific capabilities that differentiate your business, written in active language that matches how contracts are described
  • Past Performance: Relevant contract history including agency names, contract values, and brief descriptions of work performed
  • Differentiators: What makes your company unique - specialized expertise, proprietary technology, strategic locations, or key personnel
  • Company Data: DUNS/UEI, CAGE Code, NAICS codes, business size, and all applicable certifications
  • Contact Information: Primary contact, address, phone, email, and website

Past Performance: The Critical Element

Contracting officers care most about past performance. If you've done similar work successfully, you're a lower-risk choice. Your capability statement should prominently feature your most relevant contracts, even if they're commercial. No government experience yet? Highlight comparable commercial work that demonstrates your capabilities.

Design and Formatting Standards

Government capability statements follow specific formatting conventions. The document should be exactly one page, professionally designed, and easy to scan quickly. Color schemes should be professional (blue, gray, and accent colors are standard). All text must be readable without magnification.

We design capability statements that stand out while following government norms. Your document will be provided in both PDF format for email distribution and editable format (Word or Google Docs) so you can update it as you win new contracts.

Using Your Capability Statement

  • DSBS Profile: Upload to the Dynamic Small Business Search so contracting officers can find you
  • Email Campaigns: Attach when reaching out to contracting officers and prime contractors
  • Industry Days: Bring printed copies to government matchmaking events
  • Proposal Attachments: Include with bid responses as company qualifications
  • Subcontracting Outreach: Send to prime contractors seeking small business partners

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I update my capability statement?

Update after every significant contract win, certification change, or capability expansion. At minimum, review quarterly to ensure all information is current. We provide editable files so you can make updates easily.

Should I have multiple versions?

Yes, for businesses with diverse capabilities. A company that does both IT services and facilities maintenance should have separate capability statements tailored to each market. This allows you to emphasize relevant past performance for specific opportunities.

What if I have no government past performance?

Feature your commercial experience that demonstrates the same capabilities. Government buyers understand new contractors need opportunities to build federal experience. Focus on the scope and quality of work, not whether it was government or commercial.

Contract Portal Access

Finding the right government contracts is a full-time job. With opportunities posted across 2,200+ federal, state, and local procurement portals, most businesses miss contracts perfectly suited to their capabilities. Our contract portal consolidates these opportunities and delivers them to your inbox daily.

The Challenge of Finding Contracts

Government contracts are posted on multiple platforms depending on the agency, value, and type of procurement. Federal opportunities appear on SAM.gov, but also on agency-specific portals. State contracts are on state procurement websites. Local governments post on city and county portals. Large prime contractors post subcontracting opportunities on their own supplier portals.

Successful contractors spend 3-4 hours daily searching for opportunities. For small business owners already running their operations, this time investment is often impossible. Missing even one day means potentially missing your ideal contract opportunity.

What Our Portal Covers

  • Federal Contracts: SAM.gov opportunities including RFPs, RFQs, sources sought, and presolicitation notices
  • State Procurement: All 50 state procurement portals with daily updates
  • Local Government: City, county, and municipal procurement across your service area
  • Prime Contractor Opportunities: Subcontracting opportunities from major government contractors
  • Grant Opportunities: Federal and state grant programs relevant to your business

Beyond Federal: State & Local Opportunity

Most government contracting firms focus only on federal contracts. We cover all levels of government because state and local contracts often provide easier entry points for new contractors. Combined state and local government spending exceeds $2 trillion annually, with less competition than federal contracts.

How the Portal Works

Each morning, you receive an email digest of new opportunities matching your profile. You can also log into the portal anytime to search manually, save opportunities to your pipeline, and track due dates. The system includes tools for managing your opportunity pipeline from initial interest through proposal submission.

Marketing Campaigns

Winning government contracts often starts before solicitations are posted. Building relationships with contracting officers and prime contractors positions your company for opportunities that never reach full competition. Our targeted email campaigns put your capabilities in front of decision-makers.

Why Direct Marketing Works

Government contracting officers are required to conduct market research before issuing solicitations. They actively seek capable small businesses to meet their agency's contracting goals. When your capability statement lands in a contracting officer's inbox at the right time, you become part of their vendor consideration set.

Similarly, prime contractors need small business subcontractors to meet their own small business subcontracting goals. Large primes actively seek qualified partners for specific contract opportunities. A well-timed email introducing your capabilities can lead to teaming arrangements worth millions.

Campaign Targeting

Our campaigns are not mass emails. We identify and verify contacts who actually buy what you sell. Each campaign is targeted based on:

  • NAICS Code Match: Contracting officers who have purchased services in your NAICS codes
  • Geographic Relevance: Buyers in your service area or those buying for locations you serve
  • Award History: Officers with recent awards for services matching your capabilities
  • Agency Alignment: Focus on agencies with missions aligned to your expertise
  • Prime Contractor Fit: Large contractors with relevant contract vehicles needing small business partners

Contracting Officer Contact Database

Our database includes contact information for over 60,000 government buyers across federal, state, and local agencies. We maintain award history data going back to 2001, allowing us to identify who is actively buying your services and when contracts are expiring for rebid.

Campaign Execution

Each campaign includes professionally written email copy that follows government communication norms. We avoid aggressive sales language that causes government employees to disengage. Instead, we focus on introducing your capabilities and offering to provide additional information.

  • Contact List Development: We research and verify 500+ contacts per campaign based on your targeting criteria
  • Email Copy Development: Professional introduction emails that present your capabilities appropriately
  • Capability Statement Attachment: Your capability statement accompanies each email
  • Send Management: Emails sent in compliance with CAN-SPAM and government email policies
  • Response Handling: We alert you immediately to any responses for your follow-up

Types of Campaigns

  • Contracting Officer Introduction: Introduce your company to government buyers in your target agencies
  • Prime Contractor Outreach: Present your capabilities to large contractors seeking small business partners
  • Expiring Contract Alert: Reach out to buyers whose contracts with competitors are ending
  • New Capability Announcement: Announce new certifications, past performance, or service offerings

What response rate should I expect?

Government contracting is a long-game. Typical response rates are 2-5% for initial outreach. The goal is awareness - when these buyers have a need, your company is on their radar. Many contracts result from relationships built over multiple touchpoints.

Is it legal to email government employees?

Yes. Government contracting officers have public contact information specifically for vendor communication. They receive and respond to capability introductions regularly as part of their market research responsibilities.

How do I handle responses?

We forward all responses to you immediately for direct follow-up. You know your business best - when a buyer shows interest, you're the right person to continue the conversation. We can provide guidance on appropriate next steps.

Proposal Writing

You found the perfect opportunity. Now you need a proposal that wins. Government proposals follow specific formats and evaluation criteria that differ dramatically from commercial sales. Our proposal experts help you craft compliant, compelling responses that maximize your evaluation scores.

Why Expert Proposal Support Matters

Government proposals are evaluated according to specific criteria outlined in the solicitation. Evaluators use scoring matrices and adjective ratings to compare offerors. A well-written proposal that directly addresses evaluation criteria will outscore a technically superior solution poorly presented.

Common proposal mistakes include not following submission instructions (automatic disqualification), failing to address all evaluation factors, using generic boilerplate language, and not demonstrating understanding of the agency's specific needs. These errors can eliminate otherwise qualified contractors from consideration.

What We Develop

  • Compliance Matrix: Line-by-line mapping of solicitation requirements to proposal sections ensuring no requirements are missed
  • Technical Approach: Detailed methodology demonstrating how you'll perform the work and deliver results
  • Management Approach: Project management, staffing, quality control, and risk mitigation plans
  • Past Performance Volume: Compelling narratives of relevant prior contracts with reference information
  • Pricing Strategy: Competitive pricing analysis and cost volume development
  • Resumes: Government-formatted resumes for key personnel
  • Graphics: Professional visuals that communicate complex information clearly

Evaluation Criteria Focus

Every proposal we develop starts with analysis of the evaluation criteria. We identify what matters most to evaluators and structure your response to score maximum points on the highest-weighted factors. This strategic approach often matters more than having the lowest price.

Pricing Analysis

Competitive pricing requires understanding the market. We research award history for similar contracts, analyze competitor pricing patterns, and help you develop pricing that's competitive while maintaining reasonable margins. For cost-reimbursement contracts, we help develop compliant cost proposals with appropriate indirect rates.

Types of Proposals We Support

  • Request for Proposal (RFP): Full competitive proposals with technical, management, past performance, and price volumes
  • Request for Quote (RFQ): Simplified responses focused on capability and pricing
  • Sources Sought / RFI: Capability statements and responses demonstrating interest and qualifications
  • Task Order Proposals: Responses under existing contract vehicles (GSA Schedule, GWAC, BPA)
  • Oral Presentation Prep: Slide development and practice sessions for oral proposal requirements

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I engage for proposal support?

Ideally, contact us when you identify the opportunity, even before the solicitation drops. This allows time for capture planning and positioning. At minimum, engage within the first few days after solicitation release. Complex proposals need 3-4 weeks minimum; simpler RFQs can be completed in 1-2 weeks.

Do you guarantee we'll win?

No ethical proposal professional guarantees wins. Too many factors beyond proposal quality influence awards - incumbent advantage, pricing, agency preferences, and competitor strengths. We guarantee a compliant, professional proposal that maximizes your competitiveness.

What information do you need from me?

We need your technical approach to the work, past performance information, key personnel resumes, pricing inputs, and subject matter expertise for the technical solution. You know your business - we know proposals. The best results come from true collaboration.
